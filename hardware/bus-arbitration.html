Bus arbitration

Several processors on the 32X want the same memory at the same time: the 68000, the Z80, and the two SH-2s. This page explains who wins, who waits, and how the two control bits RV and FM let the 68000 and the SH-2s take turns on shared resources. Read it when your code stalls without an obvious reason, or before you hand the cartridge ROM or the video memory from one CPU to the other.

Summary#

  • Two SH-2s, one bus. The master holds it normally, and the slave gets it only after asking the master, access by accessDOC[S001-ms1][S001-ms2].
  • 68000 against SH-2. When both hit the same area at once, the SH-2 wins. Otherwise the first comer goes first and the second waitsDOC[S001-pri][S005-fcfs].
  • RV hands the cartridge ROM to the 68000 (for ROM-to-VRAM DMA). SH-2 ROM reads then wait until RV = 0DOC[S001-rv][S001-rvwait].
  • FM hands the video side to one CPU at a time. It covers the frame buffer, the overwrite image, the VDP registers and the palette. Both sides can force itDOC[S001-fm68][S001-fmsh].
  • A reported lock-up. The 32XDK repository wiki reports that simultaneous writes to one SDRAM location from both SH-2s lock the systemCOMM[S009-sd].

RV (DREQ control bit 0)#

RV is bit 0 of the DREQ control register. It is the 68000's way of taking the cartridge ROM for itself.

  • What it does. The 68000 sets RV when its program suffers from ROM waits caused by SH-2 ROM access. SH-2 ROM access then waits until RV = 0DOC[S001-rv][S001-rvwait].
  • DMA to VRAM. The R4 manual adds that RV must be 1 while doing ROM-to-VRAM DMADOC[S005-rvdma].
  • Interrupts. Sega's Developer Technical Support sent numbered 32X Technical Bulletins to developers in 1994. Bulletin 9 (September 1994) says all 68000 interrupts must be disabled while RV = 1DOC[S070-b09].
  • SH-2 side. The SH-2 can only read RVDOC[S001-rvro].
  • In the emulator. The jgenesis emulator stalls an SH-2 cartridge read loop while RV = 1; see the emulator section below.

FM (VDP access)#

FM is bit 15 of the adapter control register on the 68000 side, and the same bit in the SH-2 interrupt mask register. It says which side owns the video memory.

  • Normal hand-over. An SH-2 access to the video side waits until FM = 1. After the access FM returns to 0 and the 68000 has the VDP againDOC[S001-fmwait].
  • The same rule from the 68000 side. Page 75 of Sega's R4 manual (July 1994) states the mirror case. A 68000 access waits until FM = 0; when it ends, FM becomes 1 and the SH-2 side has access againDOC[S005-p75wait].
  • Avoiding competition. Both CPUs then wait for access to come back. Competition is avoided by handing access back when doneDOC[S001-fmboth][S001-fmhand].
  • Forced take-over from the SH-2. A 1 written from the SH-2 side takes access even in the middle of a 68000 access. What follows is not guaranteedDOC[S001-fmsh][S001-fmcut].
  • Forced take-over from the 68000. A 68000 write to FM likewise takes it from an SH-2DOC[S001-fm68].
  • Wrong-side access. The manual says elsewhere that such an access is simply ignored; see Memory map.

DMA and the bus#

  • Two SH-2 DMAs at once. One side's DMA becomes very slow until the other finishesDOC[S001-dma2]. For auto-request DMA, Sega says to mask the interrupts of both master and slaveDOC[S001-dmamask].
  • Bus release. The 32XDK wiki (the community-written wiki for 32X developers on GitHub), citing Sega material, says a cycle-stealing DMAC releases the bus after each access, and a burst transfer after each burstCOMM[S011-cs][S011-burst].

How the cartridge ROM is read#

  • The 32XDK wiki's "SH2 on the 32X" page, after its memory benchmark results, quotes comments on cartridge ROM reads by Chilly Willy, a GenDev forum member. He says the SH-2 bus controller reads ROM as non-burst single readsCOMM[S011-single].
  • Chilly Willy adds that the reads are stretched while the arbitrator gives the bus to the 68000 or the other SH-2COMM[S011-arb].

Techniques#

  • Copy hot ROM data to SDRAM. Load cartridge data the SH-2s read often into SDRAM first, to stop fighting the 68000 for ROMDOC[S001-sdcopy].
  • Measured contention costs are described on The SH-2 on the 32X and in the optimization section.

Pitfalls#

SDRAM write collisions between the SH-2s can lock up the system, according to the 32XDK wikiCOMM[S009-sd][S009-lock].

ROM data at $1070-$1073, $2070-$2073 and $3070-$3073 is "not normally read" with RV = 1DOC[S092-rv][S092-a][S092-b].

  • Sega's Technical Bulletin 12 (September 1994) lists these 12 bytes and gives no explanation. The 32XDK wiki repeats the listCOMM[S009-rv1][S009-rv2].
  • Mars Check, Sega's 32X hardware test program in the SDK, points the other way for the first 4 bytes. Its ROM-to-VRAM DMA test runs with RV = 1 and reads ROM $000400-$0013FFCODE[S210-src].
  • The test compares every word in VRAM with the cartridgeCODE[S210-cmp]. So it expects $1070-$1073 to arrive unchangedCODE[S210-exp][S210-prac].
  • The test does not cover $2070-$2073 or $3070-$3073CODE[S210-scope].
  • The bulletin and Sega's own test therefore disagree on the first 4 bytes; see where sources disagree.

RV survives a soft reset.

  • In the GenDev thread "State of RV Flag during Soft Reset" (2023), Chilly Willy says RV is not reset. His own 68000 start-up code clears RV and waits for the 32X to rebootCOMM[S111-rv][S111-init].
  • The 68000 hardware init code of d32xr, the GitHub source of Doom 32X Resurrection, also clears RV before it waits for the master SH-2's M_OKCODE[S088-clrrv].

Save RAM and RV.

  • FRAM on the cartridge needs RV = 1 to be read or written. SRAM does not care about RV, Sega's Technical Bulletin 7 (September 1994) saysDOC[S145-fram][S145-sram].
  • Charles MacDonald's test of the cartridge connector signals points the other way for 68000 writes. He posted it in the GenDev forum thread "Some 32X cart port questions" (2012): with RV = 0 the LWR# write strobe never assertsHW[S178-lwr].
  • Chilly Willy's save-RAM code, posted as working on a real 32X in the GenDev thread "Accessing SRAM" (2009), sets RV = 1 around the accessCODE[S182-fix][S182-rv].
  • The disagreement is described on Cartridge bank switching.

Sega CD titles.

  • The CD boot ROM's backup RAM routines need RV = 1, Sega's Technical Bulletin 33 (December 1994) saysDOC[S159-bk2].
  • Cartridge games need software measures for SH-2 code during VRES with RV = 1DOC[S159-vres].
  • The bulletin says this requirement does not apply to CD-ROM based gamesDOC[S159-cd].

TAS always goes to the bus.

  • Sega's Technical Bulletin 23 (November 1994) says an SH-2 TAS instruction is performed cache-through, even on a cached addressDOC[S093-ct].
  • The same bulletin says bus ownership cannot pass between master and slave while the TAS runsDOC[S093-bus].

A VRES reset with RV = 1 can leave the Mega Drive side wrong after reset.

  • Sega's Technical Bulletin 6 (September 1994) describes the problemDOC[S090-rv].
  • From development board Ver. 2.1, the bulletin says, the 32X resets itself through the master SH-2's watchdog when a VRES interrupt finds RV = 1DOC[S090-wdt].
  • Each application must decide in its VRES handler whether to resetDOC[S090-app].

Two SH-2 DMAs at once: one runs very slowly until the other finishesDOC[S001-dma2].

FM changes hands mid-access. Follow-on behaviour is not guaranteedDOC[S001-fmcut].

  • Sega's Mars Check FM test does not write FM while the other side is accessing the VDPCODE[S189-fmlim2].
  • The same Mars Check test has the 68000 take FM before it touches the paletteCODE[S188-fm].

68000 interrupts must be off while RV = 1DOC[S070-b09].

  • d32xr's 68000 code masks interrupts (#$2700) before it sets RV and unmasks after it clears RVCODE[S088-rvints][S088-rvints2].
  • So does Chilly Willy's working save-RAM codeCODE[S182-fix][S182-ints].
  • Sega's Mars Check does the same around its RV = 1 DMA. It copies the routine to work RAM and runs it from thereCODE[S210-ram]. It runs with the interrupt mask at 7CODE[S210-mask].

Tested on the jgenesis emulator#

jgenesis is a Mega Drive and 32X emulator whose source is on GitHub. These results come from its version 0.14.1. Nobody has confirmed them on a real 32X yet.

  • RV = 1 was set by a byte write to $A15107 or a word write to $A15106 (the 68000 reads $0001 back).
  • An SH-2 cartridge read loop almost stops while the 68000 holds RV = 1. It made 10 and 12 reads in the RV window, against 1,290 in the same window of a no-RV control. It read no wrong data and continued after RV = 0EMU:jgenesis core 0.14.1[T110-cols][T110-rb][T110-stop][T110-resume].
  • That matches the manual's "waits", not a hang.

Where sources disagree#

  • Wrong-side access to the video side. The manual says it is ignored in one place and held until FM = 1 in another; see Memory map.
  • Does on-chip I/O wait for the other bus master? Hitachi's SH7604 hardware manual, the SH-2 chip's own manual, and Sega's Technical Bulletin 32 (December 1994) disagree; see The SH-2 on the 32X.

Open questions#

Nobody has published a console test of the following pitfalls.

  • Do simultaneous writes to one SDRAM location from both SH-2s really lock the system, and under which timing?
  • Does the Sega CD boot ROM's backup RAM routine fail with RV = 0?
  • Can bus ownership pass between master and slave during an SH-2 TAS?
  • What state does a VRES with RV = 1 leave the Mega Drive side in, on a board without the watchdog reset?
  • How much slower is one SH-2 DMA while the other SH-2 runs its own?
